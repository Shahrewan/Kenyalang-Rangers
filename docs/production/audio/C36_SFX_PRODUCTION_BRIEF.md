# C36-A · Sound-effect audit and production brief

26 November 2026 · Kenyalang Rangers · baseline `24b4aa8` · Godot 4.7.2 · Android first, Windows desktop second

This brief commissions **one-shot sound effects** for the production game. Use the JSON manifest for filenames, triggering conditions, priority, and delivery requirements. Batch A covers interface and progression. Batch B covers battlefield actions and tactical notices. The current procedural player and existing Android build remain the baseline for playback, lifecycle, and save compatibility.

## Direction for ChatGPT Work

- Use a soft, tactical, slightly playful vocabulary that matches the W29 cutouts. Favor damped wood, cloth, small springs, compact digital chirps, and warm mallet tones. Keep a distinct attack, navigation, reward and warning character. Make ally and enemy source families distinguishable in the middle frequencies commonly heard on phone speakers.
- Main interface should feel light and immediate. Keep cancellation neutral and rejection clear without a sharp buzzer. Confirm purchases with small physical actions such as a stamp or latch. Treat progression, recruitment and quiz results warmly. Keep Restoration sounds tactile and sparse. The first hornbill visitor is a short, original stylized animal call.
- Battlefield basics use discreet, dry attacks. Round off striking transients and keep rattles short. Use a stylized mechanical release for ranged attacks, padded contact for melee, and different soft accents for support, repair and skill readiness. Use one strong onset for a special attack rather than a layer for every target. Warnings and boss beats should read clearly during a busy wave through rhythm and timbre. Keep vivid phases distinct from ordinary hits.
- Result jingles are self-contained, brief, and share one game vocabulary. Make ordinary and perfect Story success distinct. Defeat should feel conclusive and gentle. A new trophy or committed reward can share the result space with one short accent.
- Produce original synthesized or foley clips. A third-party source needs explicit reuse permission and attribution for redistribution, edits and bundling. Record the origin, creator, tool or library, license, attribution requirement, modifications and reviewer approval for each delivered file. Keep intermediate sessions outside the runtime tree. No commercial game samples or recognizable soundtrack motifs.
- Background music, loops, continuous environmental beds, voice acting, realistic weapon blasts, gore, prolonged alarms and sub-bass-heavy cinematic effects are outside this commission. The clips contain no background bed. Keep music routing and dialogue behavior separate from these sound files.

## Technical delivery

| Item | Acceptance |
| --- | --- |
| Filename and identity | Use the exact repository-relative path in the manifest. The cue and path are lowercase snake_case. The canonical enemy field preserves `E01`–`E15` uppercase. Dispatch a variant group through its base cue. Later variants have separate file and asset IDs and retain that original group. |
| File format | WAV, signed 16-bit PCM, mono, 22,050 Hz. One-shot, no loop points, no embedded ambience. Deliver one file per manifest row. |
| Duration | UI generally 50–350 ms, basic attacks 100–600 ms, skills/construction 300–1,500 ms, warnings 500–1,500 ms, results 1,500–3,500 ms. Use the individual target and maximum in the manifest. Some result and discovery accents vary to keep the previous visual rhythm. |
| Edits | Tight starts and endings with a short shaped envelope. Trim excess leading and trailing silence. Remove DC offset. Check the full waveform for clipping and clicks. Leave at least 2 dB of peak headroom as an initial file acceptance target. Compare perceived loudness within cue families and check on low-volume phone speakers. |
| Versions | Required `_01` files first. Optional `_02` and `_03` are reserved for frequent attacks and automatic healing. Variants have comparable level, duration and meaning. Optional unique accents may be deferred where a documented fallback already communicates the event. |
| Receipts | Return the exact delivered path, provenance, PCM format, measured duration, sample peak, channels, bit depth, size, checksum and review status. Leave the manifest measured fields empty until inspecting the delivered file. Decode each accepted file in the pinned Godot editor and preview it in its production gameplay context during integration. |

## Live runtime audit

There are 16 procedural SFX IDs and no tracked external audio clip files in the baseline. `PresentationAudio._sound` builds cached in-memory mono PCM16 at 11,025 Hz. SFX use about 100 ms per note. The player uses one effects voice and one alerts voice. A live repeat of the same stream on its voice is ignored while that stream plays. A different sound can replace the voice. Generic Button confirmation also yields while the effects voice is already playing. The original music generator is out of this commission. The shared settings default to Master 80%, Music 45%, SFX 75%. Master or SFX zero gates new cues. Backgrounding stops both SFX voices and holds music. Combat or quiz hold pauses music through the launcher and does not disable menu interactions. The Dummy audio driver suppresses normal play calls outside capture. ## Current cue compatibility

Every old ID retains its original procedural cue if the replacement is missing or invalid. Specific event routing uses the first available matching asset, then a shared asset or the original procedural cue. Legacy aliases resolve directly to the procedural leaf after an asset miss, avoiding fallback cycles. A new optional event can resolve to silence. The neutral direct replacement is useful while semantic routing is integrated incrementally.

## Mixer and Android acceptance after production

1. Keep Master and SFX at their saved levels and retain visual counterparts for all important events. Clamp presentation gain with conservative headroom. Keep UI response isolated from ordinary combat hits. Start with 8 SFX voices on Android and 12 on Windows, including two reserved voices for warnings and results. Skip stale low-priority actions when the pool is busy. Respect per-group cooldown and polyphony across variants. At dense 2× speed, coalesce same-action and same-tick variants and drop routine voices before an Integrity, Heat, Siege or boss warning. Avoid identical sound restarts.
2. Combat time remains the authority for triggering. Assets are one-shots at normal pitch. Retrigger cooldowns are monotonic audio wall time during the foreground. Pause or dialogue stops new combat events and clears stale queued routine combat. Intentional UI and the specific existing transition cue can still be heard. Background and phone interruption stop sound promptly. Returning plays only new audible actions. Never reschedule combat, spending, rewards or save logic around audio playback.
3. Integrate Batch A replacements first with procedural fallback. Check every old ID, silent/missing assets, SFX preview, master mute and keyboard, touch and Back behavior. Then connect Batch B typed signals individually. Prefer instrumentation for event counts, gating and dropped voices separate from listening review. Keep the last known-good signed APK available for rollback.
4. Use real playback in Godot to check decoding and action timing. Listen on an Android handset speaker and headphones first, then verify Windows output and settings. Exercise First Contact placement, simultaneous hits at 1× and 2×, Imbak overflow, bosses and Chaos, quiz timeout, restoration and repeated navigation. Verify offline startup, existing save update, app suspend/return, and zero audio output when muted. Ship only after proving the candidate preserves the stable build's behavior.
